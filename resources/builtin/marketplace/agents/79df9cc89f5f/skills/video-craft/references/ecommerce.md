# Ecommerce videos

Read only for product listing or sales-creative work. A one-step edit still goes directly to `stage-edit`.

## Build the sales idea from evidence

Choose the commercial structure the product supports: demonstration, comparison,
explanation or promotion. Transfer a reference's hook/proof/CTA relationship to
the user's product rather than copying unrelated selling points. Anchor SKU,
appearance, price/currency, offer conditions and CTA in supplied facts. Build
the story from useful known details; everyday implications, playful language and
creative shot ideas are welcome without turning them into new product features
or measured results. If a detail is uncertain, choose another supported angle;
ask only when it determines the requested result. Real product assets carry
product evidence; generated atmosphere does not establish efficacy.

## Script and shots

Start with the viewer's buying question and the supported product detail that
answers it. Turn that detail into something the viewer can see or understand;
generic praise such as "premium quality" or "a better life" does not explain
this product. An opening can identify the product, raise a relevant question,
show an observed result or introduce a comparison. Choose what the brief and
evidence support; a problem hook, demonstration, price and CTA are not a fixed
four-part recipe.

Before authoring the page, work out a compact shot sequence. For each shot,
connect its communication purpose, source asset or observed source moment,
framing/action, on-screen words or narration, and enough time to understand it.
Express those decisions in the existing scenes, clips and text; no extra schema
or approval step is needed. A shot should develop the product story, establish
context or give necessary reading time. Repeating a full product image with a
new slogan or tiny zoom does not by itself add information.

Distinguish what the available material can show:

- Packaging or listing images: identify the product and reveal relevant visible
  details or attributed packaging information. Packaging art is not an observed
  use demonstration. Keep missing use evidence in the handoff, not as production
  caveats in the promotional copy.
- Real product photos: use the observed shape, material and details; do not infer
  unseen parts or functionality from a still. Use the source roles and framing
  method in [design-system-importer](@skill/design-system-importer) and
  [timeline framing](@skill/video-studio/references/timeline.md) when choosing a complete view or detail.
- Use footage: select observed setup, action and result when these answer the
  buying question. For example, a sink strainer's debris capture, removal and
  cleaning can form a useful sequence if those actions are actually visible;
  do not invent fit, drainage performance or test results.
- Style references: borrow the relevant visual language, not another product's
  appearance, accessories or claims.

Choose by the control you need: cinematic product action can be a generated clip;
exact editable offer words belong in the page. Reuse the actual product references.
For work that follows a reference, use [reference-recreation.md](reference-recreation.md).
Keep words in the timeline, off the footage, so a later price or caption change
reuses the finished footage.

## Common formats

Use one when the material supports it; each has one job.

- Review or test: claim, test, evidence, one real limitation, verdict, with the
  test taking the most time. A test needs the user's footage of the product
  doing it; a generated scene can show use, not a test result.
- Unboxing: the closed package, one reveal, the reaction, the product working.
  Use the user's packaging or a plain box; never invent branded packaging.
- Demo or how-to: the setup, numbered steps each shown to its end, the result,
  following the product's real sequence.
- Try-on: before, wearing it in motion, a close detail, the fit facts. Putting
  it on happens on the cut; colour, shape and fittings stay true.
- Comparison: the same framing and light for both, and only stated facts about
  either; name no competitor without a source.
- Before and after: only with the user's real before and after; otherwise show
  the problem and the product in use.
- A presenter or creator talking to camera: follow [presenter.md](presenter.md).
- Live-stream clip (直播切片): from the user's recording, pick a self-contained
  moment where the product is shown and the offer is said, cut on whole
  sentences with [stage-decide](@skill/stage-decide), caption it and keep the
  host's own words.

## Where it runs

When the user names a platform, follow its current rules; these points are
stable:

- Douyin product video: the product within the first three seconds, as the
  main subject, shown whole, one product, the real item as listed rather than
  a generated one, no plot; 1:1, 3:4 or 9:16, best within 30 seconds.
- Amazon listing video: no prices, promotions, time-limited offers, links or
  contact details, so leave the price card out.
- TikTok Shop shoppable video: moving footage in a real setting with the
  physical product shown.
- Everywhere: no QR codes, URLs, other platforms' names or logos, or
  third-party watermarks, and the product on screen matches what ships.

## Product continuity

Map product shots and overlays to the selected SKU and evidenced product attributes. Use the existing source/reference and preserve/may-change boundaries. Keep source footage unchanged outside the requested edit; do not invent accessories, operating behavior or demonstrated results.

## Candidate inspection

After the last timing revision, compare the final narration, captions and product
actions with the supplied material, focusing on functions, operation, specs and
results. Shortening a line must preserve its factual meaning. Keep useful
interpretation and creative expression; revise the specific claim that outpaces
its source rather than flattening the whole script. User-confirmed operation can
support a proposed shoot without footage. An unconfirmed action can remain a
conditional shooting idea rather than a definite consumer promise. This is a
lightweight creative review, without a required fact table, sentence-by-sentence
citations or extra approval stage.

Review the actual sequence against the original request, not only the authored
timeline. Can a new viewer identify the product, understand the intended buying
point, and follow how the shots support it? Check the opening and its payoff,
meaningful changes within scenes and the joins between them. Use `video.sheet`
at the relevant times; after rendering, inspect the final-file frames returned
by `video.ship`. A few sampled frames cannot establish smooth motion between
them. Technical success or changing pixels does not establish a useful story.
Name the specific scene/time and corrective change for a warranted finding;
do not invent a minimum shot count, product-area ratio or mandatory transition.

Inspect the actual candidate's source product placement, locally authored labels and factual overlays at representative times and after local revisions. Compare against the selected source, including across shot boundaries when present. Check factual overlays for readability without obscuring the product. Apply video-craft's text-contrast and caption-legibility criteria to these overlays even in a one-shot edit: use a solid contrasting text fill or a suitable backing on the actual footage, not a pale fill whose thin outline alone carries the letters. Judge rendered frames; OCR text alone cannot verify contrast. If the candidate cannot be inspected or a product attribute does not match, state the unverified or mismatched result in the handoff.
