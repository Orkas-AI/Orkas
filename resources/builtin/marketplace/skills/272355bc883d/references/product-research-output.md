# Selection decision output

Lead with the recommended next action. Scale the rest to the decision rather than producing a fixed long report.

Start with a compact scope line: proposed first country | first platform | candidate set | current validation stage. Fill every decision-relevant unrestricted field with a bounded proposal and label it as proposed; a region such as Southeast Asia is context, not a first-country choice. Do not return that choice to the merchant as the next prerequisite.

State evidence status at the claim or evidence-lane level; a response may combine source-backed facts with explicit hypotheses. For each source-backed claim, give the direct returned or opened source and keep the claim within that source's market, category, metric and time scope. For a hypothesis with no observed source, omit report names, direct links, rankings, current statistics and phrases such as "data shows".

Use two clear lanes in the delivered answer: put unsourced category rationales in a section or table explicitly labeled as hypotheses, and put sourced external facts in the evidence map with direct links. Do not leave an unsourced demand, competition, compliance, conversion or return-risk rationale in an unqualified reason column.

For the external claims that affect the decision, include a compact evidence map: narrow claim | evidence status | direct source record or URL | applicable scope. In a follow-up, cite the exact prior source again when using it for a new factual claim; a remembered report title alone is not a source record.

Treat category demand, popularity and competition rationales as hypotheses unless a directly mapped source supports their exact scope. A broad market or platform source may explain channel context, but it does not establish demand for a proposed category or SKU.

Include rejected candidates when alternatives were compared. Show the decision-changing calculations, sources and adverse scenario. Separate measured facts, estimates and judgment. State the frozen comparison set and unobserved coverage.

Choose one current stage from the unresolved decision and the inputs already available: category comparison, supplier/sample qualification, inventory demand trial or replenishment. Category comparison returns conditional candidates and the evidence that would separate them. Supplier qualification can use a quoted sample, sample inspection or existing inspection evidence. An inventory demand trial commits inventory to observe sales, so it is unlocked only when the merchant's commitment ceiling, compatible landed costs and fulfillment basis, and an arrival/observation window are available. A production MOQ is a constraint on a bulk order, not the default size of every experiment. For an untested supplier, a sample-term request can therefore precede inventory sizing, without claiming either action has occurred.

When the inputs unlock an inventory trial, derive it from the comparison's constraints: upfront committed cash, remaining reserve, arrival date and time left to observe sales. For a fixed total budget, show one executable base allocation and its arithmetic total. Use ranges only when they help the decision, and then show both the minimum and maximum totals within the ceiling. A staged trial can preserve room for uncertain costs; separate quoted costs from proposed reserves. Before those inputs exist, make the current quotation or sample step executable. Describe the locked inventory stage only as an advancement gate: list the required inputs and the formula or rule that will convert them into a quantity. Do not include a proposed inventory quantity or range, total inventory spend or SKU allocation percentage, even under words such as "after", "then", "small batch" or "illustrative".

Work backward from the next inventory decision. For each proposed demand trial, calculate selling days = decision deadline minus arrival day, cash committed, cash left and inventory still exposed if sales are zero. Upfront exposure is not automatically a realized loss: identify recoverable stock and unknown salvage value. Compare one adverse cost or sales scenario with the base case before choosing the commitment.

Make the current stage executable from observations. For category comparison, specify the comparison set, fields and decision rule. For supplier/sample qualification, specify the quote or sample, observable inspection criteria and advancement rule. End an earlier-stage plan with the inputs and calculation method needed to unlock inventory sizing. For an unlocked, sized inventory trial, specify quantity/spend ceiling | dated observation window | paid units and realized contribution to measure | continuation boundary | action otherwise. Choose a numerical order or sell-through target appropriate to the available selling days and inventory, label it as a proposed experiment assumption, and explain the risk it bounds. Pair that target with the economic boundary; positive unit margin alone says nothing about inventory moving. If the observation misses either boundary, state the smaller follow-up or pause and remaining cash/stock exposure. A successful small trial supports another bounded commitment, not proven market-wide demand.

If no total commitment ceiling was supplied, do not turn an illustrative amount into the recommended plan. Keep the plan executable with non-monetary scope and gates, or present a monetary scenario only as conditional on the merchant choosing that ceiling.

For an unlocked inventory trial, let `C` be the merchant's commitment ceiling, `R` the chosen reserve, `L` the compatible landed unit cost, `D` the decision deadline and `A` the arrival date. The maximum order quantity is `floor((C - R) / L)` and the selling window is `D - A`; calculate the actual values from the supplied inputs, then choose and label a paid-unit target that bounds the merchant's stated risk. Without those inputs, return this formula as the advancement rule rather than substituting illustrative numbers. Supplier inspection instead needs observable quality criteria and a sample or inspection cost ceiling, not a fabricated sales target. These are recommendations within the decision, not additional approval stages.
