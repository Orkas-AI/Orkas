# Content series and response copy

Use only the requested extension. Both use the working brief, evidence policy
and editing pipeline in the entrypoint; neither starts a separate fact-checking
or writing process.

## Content calendar

Resolve the date range, cadence, audience, goal, content pillars, key dates,
available material and production/review capacity from supplied context. Default
nonblocking choices explicitly; do not invent a campaign event or approved claim.

Balance recurring pillars, platform rhythm, key dates and flexible slots against
actual capacity. Give each slot a specific topic, hook or reader question,
format, desired reader action, evidence needs and media requirements. Avoid
repeating one idea under slightly different titles. Reuse supplied facts across
a series without making them broader or more certain in later posts.

Deliver the calendar, usually as:

| Date/slot | Platform/placement | Pillar | Format | Topic/hook | Reader action | Source/media needs |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Include cadence, pillar mix and material assumptions when they explain the plan;
note flexible slots and production/review risks only when relevant. If the user
requests finished posts as well as placement, deliver every requested draft:
a table of topics is not a substitute. Apply the normal drafting and format
rules to each post rather than returning only hooks or outlines.

Calendar authoring does not authorize scheduling, reminders or publishing.

## Comment, complaint and crisis replies

Resolve the original message, surrounding context, platform, prior replies,
supporting facts and the account's approved position. Separate an allegation
from a verified event; missing evidence is not proof for either party.

Assess the issue as a complaint, fair criticism, misunderstanding, bad-faith
attack, coordinated attack, or legal/safety/privacy/regulatory issue. Use a
proportionate severity of low, medium, high, critical when a risk assessment is
useful. Do not assert motive or coordination without evidence. Decide whether
a response helps before escalating a public disagreement.

Draft a reply that addresses the actual concern, matches the supplied voice,
states only supported facts and offers an authorized next step. Do not expose
personal data, invent an investigation or resolution, admit unverified liability,
or make commitments on the account's behalf; do not promise refunds or remedies
without an approved basis. Keep private case details out of public copy.

For an ordinary comment, return the copy-ready reply directly. For a complaint
or crisis requiring decisions, put the draft first, then the needed assessment:
issue/severity, whether to respond, material unknowns, approved-position gaps,
owner/expert review, and any monitoring suggestions. These are recommendations,
not a claim that monitoring or incident handling has started. Crisis, legal,
refund, privacy, safety, regulatory, media and minors-related response copy
requires the responsible human's approval; do not publish or send it.
