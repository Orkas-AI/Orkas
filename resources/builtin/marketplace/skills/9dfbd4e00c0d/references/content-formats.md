# Editorial content formats

Use these as channel-aware defaults after the working brief is established. Preserve the evidence policy across adaptations.

## Shared rules

- Match structure to the reader's job, not to a fixed section count.
- If the exact format is not listed below, infer it from placement, audience, reader job, and desired action. Borrow the closest useful conventions, then adapt them; do not return a generic explanation of the topic.
- Make the title and opening promise only what the body delivers.
- Put the strongest relevant material early; keep background proportional.
- Use examples, tables, checklists, or visuals only when they compress real complexity.
- Keep calls to action specific, proportionate, and compatible with the evidence.
- Never add SEO claims, customer outcomes, testimonials, or product comparisons without support.

## Select the format

| Format | Opening and shape | Evidence expectation | Ending |
|---|---|---|---|
| social post | specific headline and tension-led hook; short, scannable development through concrete situations | verify checkable claims; use realistic generic scenarios without inventing users, results, or metrics | one useful takeaway or low-friction action; hashtags only when they fit |
| research explainer | answer the central question early; define scope; move from mechanism to implications | cite material facts and distinguish consensus, dispute, and inference | bounded synthesis and remaining uncertainty |
| blog/article | strong reader problem or observation; flexible narrative or argument | cite checkable material claims without turning prose into a source dump | resolve the promise or give a concrete next step |
| newsletter | specific subject/preheader; quick payoff; scannable sections with editorial voice | link claims close to their use; distinguish reporting from author take | one primary action or memorable final observation |
| tutorial/how-to | outcome, prerequisites, version/date, then ordered steps and checkpoints | verify commands, UI labels, versions, and expected results | validation, troubleshooting, and next safe step |
| case study | context, constraint, intervention, measured result, limits | separate customer-provided claims from verified metrics; define baseline and time window | transferable lesson, not guaranteed outcome |
| thought leadership/opinion | explicit thesis and stakes; strongest argument and credible counterargument | facts remain sourced; label analysis and prediction as such | implication, decision, or testable next question |
| SEO-oriented article | match search intent and answer core query early; use descriptive headings | verify all claims; cover necessary subquestions rather than keywords | concise summary/next action; no filler FAQ |

## Social post

When the user gives only a goal, choose a platform-neutral default and write the post directly. Do not ask the user to provide a detailed brief unless the missing information would materially change the artifact.

- Write a distinct, benefit-specific headline. Follow with an opening that names a recognizable tension, decision, or moment; do not open with a generic definition of the topic.
- Develop two to four concrete situations. Show what the reader is doing, what becomes easier, and what still requires judgment. A comma-separated feature list is not development.
- For generic products or categories, use realistic workflow examples without inventing a brand, customer, testimonial, ranking, metric, or personal experience.
- Make every paragraph earn its place: hook, scenario, implication, or action. Remove abstract transitions such as “becoming a digital colleague” unless the surrounding copy explains an observable behavior.
- Vary sentence and paragraph length around emphasis. Avoid stacking slogans, forced “不是……而是……” contrasts, and interchangeable future-of-work conclusions.
- End with one proportional action or question. Add three to five hashtags only when they help the implied channel; hashtags do not replace a closing action.
- For a platform-neutral Chinese social article with no requested length, default to roughly 450–700 Chinese characters. Shorten only when the user asks for a caption, micro-post, or strict platform limit.

## Research explainer

- Define the exact question, population, period, and terms that change interpretation.
- Separate `what happened`, `why it may have happened`, and `what follows`.
- Prefer original studies/data and explain methodology limits in reader-sized language.
- Avoid a false neutral midpoint when evidence is asymmetric.

## Newsletter

- Always render a distinct subject as the first non-empty line, using a clear `Subject:` / `主题：` label or heading treatment. Do not omit it because the body is short, and do not count the first body sentence as a subject. Keep it accurate at inbox distance; avoid curiosity gaps the body cannot repay.
- Use a short opening that earns attention through relevance, not greetings or generic scene-setting.
- Let voice guide selection and interpretation, but attribute external facts.
- Use one main call to action. Secondary links should not compete with it.
- Preserve the source's publication/status language exactly. Do not turn “report at /path” into “report published” or another unsupported state.
- Count the requested primary CTA semantically and visually. Render the CTA label and its destination once; do not repeat the destination in body copy unless the user explicitly requests a secondary mention. When a source sentence exists only to state “full report at /path,” merge that sentence into the single CTA and remove the separate body occurrence. Finish with a literal destination count of one.

## Tutorial

- State environment, permissions, versions, inputs, and destructive risks before the first consequential step.
- Give each step an action, expected result, and verification when failure would otherwise be ambiguous.
- Put optional branches and troubleshooting near the step they affect.
- Do not claim a procedure works unless it was tested or clearly label it as unverified.

## Case study

- Identify who supplied each claim and whether it was independently verified.
- Quantify baseline, intervention window, measurement method, and denominator.
- Name concurrent changes that weaken causal attribution.
- Avoid turning one result into a universal promise.

## Thought leadership or opinion

- State a contestable thesis, not a topic description.
- Distinguish evidence, interpretation, value judgment, and forecast.
- Steelman the strongest relevant counterargument before responding.
- Prefer a non-obvious implication or decision rule over trend language.
- Do not imitate a named author's distinctive living style; use supplied voice traits or broad characteristics instead.

## SEO-oriented article

- Infer the dominant intent: learn, compare, decide, troubleshoot, or act.
- Answer the main query directly before expanding into supporting questions.
- Use terms readers naturally use; do not repeat phrases to satisfy an imagined keyword density.
- Write headings as information architecture, not as a list of near-duplicate queries.
- Add FAQ only for real adjacent questions not already answered.
- Keep metadata accurate: title, description, and snippet language must be supported by the body.

## Adapt existing content

When adapting rather than researching from scratch:

1. Freeze the source article's claim set, evidence, certainty, and disclosures.
2. Rebuild the reader promise, opening, pacing, headings, and call to action for the target channel.
3. Shorten by removing lower-priority material, not by dropping qualifiers from surviving claims.
4. Re-check citations after moving or combining paragraphs.
5. Mark any newly required fact or example as a research gap; do not improvise it.
